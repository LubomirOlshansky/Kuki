Getting started

Which sensors work?

Kūki is made for the IKEA ALPSTUGA air-quality sensor. It also works with any other sensor in Apple Home that reports CO₂, PM2.5 or air quality; temperature and humidity are read too when the sensor has them.

If you have more than one, choose which one Kūki follows in Settings › Sensor.

What do I need?
  • An air sensor in the Home app, such as the IKEA ALPSTUGA. Add it once with an iPhone or iPad. Kūki reads it through Apple Home; no IKEA hub, app or cloud is needed.
  • For the Mac: a home hub with Thread (HomePod mini, HomePod 2nd generation, or an Apple TV 4K with Thread). See below.
  • A Mac with macOS 15 or later, or an iPhone or iPad with iOS 18 or later.

The first time you open Kūki it asks for access to your Home. If you said no, allow it under Privacy & Security in Settings (HomeKit on iPhone and iPad, Home in System Settings on the Mac).

Do I need a HomePod or Apple TV? Does the Mac need my iPhone?

The ALPSTUGA talks Matter over Thread, a low-power radio network. Something with a Thread radio has to bridge it into Apple Home:

  • Mac: yes, you need a home hub with Thread: a HomePod mini, a HomePod (2nd generation) or an Apple TV 4K with Thread. Macs have no Thread radio, so the Mac reaches the sensor through the hub.
  • iPhone 15 Pro or later: Apple Home on these iPhones can talk to Thread accessories directly, without a hub, while the iPhone is near the sensor. A hub still makes the connection steadier.
  • Older iPhones and iPads: need the hub, like the Mac.

The Mac doesn’t need your iPhone after setup. Your iPhone’s Thread radio only serves the iPhone itself; it doesn’t act as a bridge for other devices. With a hub, the Mac records on its own, around the clock, whether your iPhone is home or not.

Kūki says “No air sensor found in Apple Home”
  • Open the Home app on the same device and check that the sensor is there and shows a reading. If the Home app can’t reach it, Kūki can’t either.
  • Make sure Kūki may use your Home: Privacy & Security › HomeKit (Home on the Mac).
  • If you have more than one home, the sensor must be in a home this device can see.
  • On a Mac or an older iPhone, check that your HomePod or Apple TV is on and set as the home hub (Home app › Home Settings › Home Hubs & Bridges).
I don’t have a sensor yet. Can I try Kūki?

Yes. Turn on Settings › Demo sensor. Kūki then shows a simulated sensor, and you can add three days of demo history to explore History and Export. Delete the demo data from the same section when you’re done.

Recording

Why does my iPhone only record while Kūki is open?

Apple Home only gives sensor values to an app that’s active. When Kūki is in the background on iPhone or iPad, Apple Home stops answering it, so nothing can be recorded. History therefore shows the times Kūki was open, and gaps are drawn as no data rather than guessed.

For continuous data:

  • Let a Mac record around the clock (see below) and turn on iCloud Sync, so your iPhone shows the same history.
  • Or leave a spare iPhone or iPad on a charger in Station Mode: a dim, always-awake screen that keeps Kūki in front.
How does the Mac record 24/7?

On the Mac, Kūki runs from the menu bar, which keeps it active, so it records around the clock, even with its window closed.

  • Keep Settings › Show in Dock off. With a Dock icon, Kūki becomes a normal app and pauses in the background.
  • Turn on Open at Login so recording resumes after a restart.
  • Your Mac must be awake. Recording pauses while it sleeps.
What do “Paused” and “Reaching your sensor…” mean?

Paused means Kūki can’t hear the sensor right now, usually because the app isn’t in front, and it tells you why instead of pretending the sensor vanished.

Reaching your sensor… appears when you come back to Kūki while Apple Home reconnects. It usually takes 5–15 seconds. Kūki never records or alerts on an old value.

How do rooms work?

Tap the room name at the top of Now and choose where the sensor is. History is kept for each room, so you can compare them.

Right after a move, the CO₂ cell needs a few minutes to adapt. Those minutes count as settling and are left out of comparisons; set how long in Settings › Recording.

How do I change the CO₂ alert?

In Settings › Alert, set your limit (1000 ppm by default, the usual “open a window” line) and turn the wind-chime sound and the notification on or off. The fūrin rings once per stuffy spell, then stays quiet until the air has clearly recovered.

iCloud and your data

How do I turn iCloud Sync on or off?

Use Settings › iCloud Sync on each device. When it’s on, the history each device records, your rooms and which room the sensor is in sync between your devices through your private iCloud, and the latest reading reaches your other devices’ widgets, Lock Screen and alerts.

Sync needs the same Apple Account with iCloud turned on on each device. Turning it off stops syncing; what is already in iCloud stays there until you remove it in Settings › Apple Account › iCloud › Manage Storage.

How do I export my data?

iPhone and iPad: open the Export tab, choose a period (24 hours, 7 days, 30 days or all), then:

  • For Claude or ChatGPT: a Markdown brief with context, a summary per room and the data averaged to fit a chat. Share the file, or copy the text and paste it into the conversation.
  • Spreadsheet (CSV): every sample at full resolution, for Numbers, Excel or Google Sheets.

Mac: choose File › Export CSV… (⇧⌘E) or File › Export for AI… (⌥⇧⌘E). Both export the period History is showing.

How do I delete my data?

In Settings › Data. Readings live only on your devices (and in your private iCloud if sync is on). The developer never receives any of it. See the privacy policy.

Technical details

How does a reading get from the sensor to Kūki?

ALPSTUGA → Matter over Thread → Thread border router (your HomePod or Apple TV, or the iPhone’s own radio) → Apple Home → HomeKit → Kūki.

Kūki reads five HomeKit values from any accessory that has them: carbon dioxide level, current temperature, current relative humidity, PM2.5 density and air quality. An accessory counts as an air sensor when it reports CO₂, PM2.5 or air quality. Kūki only reads; it never changes your accessories.

How does the Mac app keep recording in the background?

Apple Home only answers an app that’s active, on the Mac as on the iPhone. Kūki for Mac is a Mac Catalyst app with a small AppKit plug-in for the menu bar item. Without a Dock icon, it runs as a menu bar app and keeps a tiny invisible window open, which keeps it active, so Apple Home keeps answering, day and night.

Turn on Show in Dock and it becomes an ordinary app that pauses in the background. A sleeping Mac doesn’t record.

How often does Kūki record, and how fresh is a value?
  • It records every 1 minute by default; choose 1, 2, 5 or 10 in Settings › Recording.
  • Every value carries the time Apple Home last really reported it. A value older than 10 minutes is never recorded or alerted on, and gaps are drawn as no data.
  • After you move the sensor, the first 10 minutes (adjustable) count as settling, because the CO₂ cell needs time to adapt. They’re recorded but left out of comparisons and alerts.
What do the levels mean, and when does the alert ring?
  • Clear air under 800 ppm · Fine 800–999 · Getting stale 1000–1399 · Stuffy 1400 and above. Outdoor air is about 420 ppm.
  • The alert limit is 1000 ppm by default (600–2000 ppm). The fūrin rings once when CO₂ reaches it, then stays quiet until CO₂ drops 100 ppm below the limit, and never more than once every 20 minutes.
  • The sound is on by default; notifications are off until you turn them on. In the Mac menu bar, ◐ marks stale air and ● stuffy air.
Where is my data stored, and what does iCloud Sync send?

Readings are stored on the device that recorded them. With iCloud Sync on, Kūki uses your private iCloud database (CloudKit): each device’s history in hourly chunks, your rooms and which room the sensor is in, plus the latest reading so your other devices’ widgets, Live Activity and alerts can follow a Mac that records around the clock. The developer can’t see any of it.

What’s in the exports?
  • CSV: every sample at full resolution: time, room, CO₂, temperature, humidity, PM2.5, and whether the sensor was still settling.
  • Markdown for Claude or ChatGPT: a short brief with context, a summary per room and the data averaged so it fits in a chat.
How do widgets and the Live Activity stay current?

They show the latest reading Kūki has, from this device or, with iCloud Sync, from your Mac. When a reading gets old they fade instead of showing it as current. The Live Activity, Dynamic Island and StandBy are iPhone features.
